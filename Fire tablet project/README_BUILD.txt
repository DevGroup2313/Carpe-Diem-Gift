CARPE DIEM FOR THE FIRE TABLET
==============================

Build 1060, 29 October 2026. The About panel inside the app ends with
"Build 1060 · 528171060".
The app's own version is 1060 too; Android usually shows it on the
app's information page (on a Fire tablet: Settings, then Apps &
Notifications).

Carpe Diem on the Fire tablet is a real Android app. It is one file,
carpe-diem.apk. It shows the seal on the home screen, opens full screen,
and runs completely offline: everything it needs is inside the app. It
is also allowed to use the internet, so it can reach the network
whenever something in it can make use of a connection. It needs no app
store, no account and no browser.


NEW IN BUILD 1060
  The proposal no longer carries a date of its own. Earlier builds set a target date
  42 days after the first start, and three lines wrote "16 September 2026" whatever
  was chosen. Your desk's proposal date is cleared once, as you asked, and no edition
  ever chooses one for you: choose one if and when it suits you, in Candidatus,
  Proposal, "Rename the proposal". That key now opens the proposal's own details: its
  working title, its target date (with a key to clear it) and its sections' names. The
  curriculum's names have their own panel, "Rename the curriculum". The first-draft
  milestone follows the proposal's date, and a passed date is said calmly, once.

  The side buttons inside a floating window are drawn as the main window's are,
  without the stacked dark shadows, which were slow and showed as dark squares on a
  Mac; they keep their lift, the bell's ring and the hourglass's turn. The 💭 there
  shows the number of thoughts too, and that number is one flat colour, as the bell's.

  Timed reminders ("In five minutes: ...", the two-minute clock, a thought asking to
  come back, and Custodia's pause) come in Carpe Diem's own blue card with a fine gold
  rim, by day and by night. Custodia's card and its keys are styled again.

  On Windows 10 the side buttons show Microsoft's flat emoji drawings, as on Windows
  11, without the heavy black outline.

  While Carpe Diem starts, the loading page says that the page is still being painted
  and how far it has got, in English or French; after a long wait it offers to start
  again. Refresh (the ↻ key beside Display, the first row of the Display menu, F5, or
  Ctrl+Shift+R, Command+Shift+R on a Mac) saves the work of every open room, then
  starts Carpe Diem again.

  The About panel is shorter. The workflows that build the apps on GitHub use actions
  that run on Node.js 24, so the Node.js 20 notice no longer appears.

EARLIER, IN BUILD 1048
  The book spines, mended. On Home's Current readings and on every shelf of
  the Library, each spine had white panels over its leather by day, and navy
  panels by night. The cause was a rule from build 1047 that gives the panels
  which open over the page a solid surface. It named a class, by mistake, that
  the spines' label panels also carry, so it painted them too. The spines are
  not part of that rule any more; they are leather and gilt again, in every
  mode.

  The side buttons, without the dark shadow. Each emoji in the side column
  (the bell, the 💭, the moon or sun, the hourglass, the notepad) carried three
  or four stacked shadows, black and heavy by night. Shadows of this kind are
  redrawn on every frame while a button moves, which made the column slow. They
  are gone, by day and by night. The blue dot beside the 💭 is gone too; the
  number of thoughts in the Inbox is in the 💭's name when the pointer rests
  on it.

  The introduction door moves and resizes again. The old fault was found:
  the door's size was set up only while the app was loading, during about the
  first two seconds. A door built after that (reopened from the seal, rebuilt
  by the language button, or built late on a slower device) kept a width rule
  that overruled every pull on its edges, so it could not be resized. Each door
  is now set up as it is built. The door can also be moved: press any empty part
  of it, or with a finger its top (the seal, the name and the row of buttons),
  and drag it anywhere on the screen. A pulled edge now follows the pointer,
  while the opposite edge stays where it is, and the edges are easier to catch
  with a finger. Moved down, the door keeps its foot on the screen. It
  remembers its place and its size. A double-click on an empty part of the door
  puts it back in the middle, at its first size. Nothing shows on the door for
  any of this.

  Support moves to the Overview, and folds away. The supports for an
  AuDHD mind no longer sit on Home, where they crowded its layout. They live
  together in one section, Support, at the top of the Overview page: your next
  step with Smaller steps, Just start and the Focus room; the time you can see;
  the routines, the Inbox, Low energy today and Calm. Press the section's
  heading, or Fold away, and it folds to a single line; press Show and it comes
  back. It stays as you left it. Home keeps its own look.

  The 💭 beside the bell, and the quill line. The 💭 has left the top bar
  for the side column, under the bell and above the moon, where it looks and
  moves like its neighbours. Its
  panel is now the quill line you chose: one quiet line with a gold rule that
  grows under the words as you write, Keep, Inbox, and a close button, "Close

  app: the panel springs open, and each button lifts when the pointer rests on

  paper with a fine gold edge in Light, and the night navy with a fine gold edge
  in Dark. Its buttons stay raised: each rises a little when the pointer rests
  on it and sinks when pressed. The panel now works like a sticky note: it
  grows as you write, a line at a time, up to about two thirds of the screen,
  and then scrolls with no scroll bar in view. Enter starts a new line; press
  Keep to keep the thought. It can be moved: press any part of it that is not the line or a button, and drag it
  anywhere on the screen, with a mouse or a finger; it opens there the next
  time. A new button beside the close button, "Minimise the thought panel",
  folds it into the 💭 and keeps what you were writing. Press the 💭 and it
  comes back with your words, ready to go on. The close button still closes it
  without keeping the words.

PART 1. INSTALL THE APP ON THE TABLET
-------------------------------------

Step 1. Allow the install (once only).
  On the tablet open Settings, then Security & Privacy, then
  "Apps from Unknown Sources". Turn it on for the app you will use to
  open the APK: Files, Docs or Silk Browser. The wording differs a
  little between Fire OS versions.

Step 2. Put carpe-diem.apk on the tablet. Choose ONE way.

  By USB cable from a Mac:
    A Mac cannot see a Fire tablet's storage without a helper app,
    because the tablet uses a connection type (MTP) that macOS does not
    read by itself. Install a free MTP app on the Mac. OpenMTP is one
    free choice; please check its website for the macOS versions it
    supports before you install it. Connect the tablet with its cable,
    unlock it, and if the tablet asks, choose "File transfer". Copy
    carpe-diem.apk into the tablet's Download folder.

  By USB cable from a Windows PC:
    Connect the tablet and unlock it. It appears in File Explorer under
    "This PC". Copy carpe-diem.apk into its Download folder.

  Without a cable:
    Email carpe-diem.apk to yourself and open the email on the tablet,
    or open the repository's Releases page in Silk (see Part 2) and tap
    carpe-diem.apk.

Step 3. Install.
  On the tablet open Files (or Docs), go to Download, tap
  carpe-diem.apk, then tap Install. Carpe Diem appears with your apps.

UPDATING LATER
  Every APK made from this project is signed with the same permanent
  key (app/carpe-diem.keystore). A newer APK therefore installs over the
  old one and keeps everything you have written. Just repeat Steps 2
  and 3 with the new file.

IF THE TABLET SAYS "App not installed"
  This happens once if an OLDER Carpe Diem is on the tablet that was
  built before the permanent key existed. Its signature differs, so
  Android refuses to replace it. Do this, in this order:
    1. Open the old Carpe Diem and export a backup of your work.
       Save it somewhere you can find it, such as Download.
    2. Uninstall the old Carpe Diem (press and hold its icon).
    3. Install carpe-diem.apk as in Step 3.
    4. Open Carpe Diem and import the backup.
  After this, every future update installs over the top.


PART 2. REBUILD THE APK YOURSELF ON GITHUB
------------------------------------------

GitHub can build the APK for you on its own computers. You install
nothing. You upload this project once, and every time you upload a
change, GitHub builds a fresh carpe-diem.apk and puts it on the
repository's Releases page.

Step 1. Make a repository.
  Sign in at github.com. Click the "+" at the top right, then
  "New repository". Give it a name, for example carpe-diem-fire.
  Choose Private. Click "Create repository".
  (You may also use your existing repository Carpe-this-diem-fire-tablet.)

Step 2. Upload the project.
  On the new repository's page click "uploading an existing file".
  Open the folder "Fire tablet project" from this package, select
  everything inside it, and drag it all onto the page. Wait until every
  file has finished uploading, then click "Commit changes".
  The app folder alone holds a 20 MB file, so this can take a minute.

Step 3. Add the build recipe. THIS STEP MATTERS.
  The recipe lives in a folder called .github. A Mac hides every name
  that begins with a full stop, so this folder is almost always left
  behind when you drag files. Without it GitHub never builds anything.
  Add it by hand. It takes one minute:
    a. On the repository page click "Add file", then "Create new file".
    b. In the name box type exactly:
           .github/workflows/build-apk.yml
       (GitHub turns each "/" into a folder as you type.)
    c. Open GITHUB_WORKFLOW_paste_this.txt from this package. Copy all
       of it and paste it into the large box on GitHub.
    d. Click "Commit changes", then "Commit changes" again.
  If the .github folder did upload in Step 2, GitHub will say the file
  already exists. That is fine; skip this step.

Step 4. Wait for the build.
  Click the "Actions" tab. A run called "Build Carpe Diem APK" appears.
  A yellow dot means it is working. It takes about three to six
  minutes. A green tick means it worked.

Step 5. Get the APK.
  On the repository's main page, on the right, click "Releases", then
  "Carpe Diem for Fire tablet". Tap or click carpe-diem.apk to download
  it. On the tablet you can do this directly in Silk, as long as you are
  signed in to GitHub there, because the repository is private.
  The same APK is also on the run's own page under "Artifacts", as a zip.

To rebuild later, for example with a newer carpe_diem.html:
  Open the repository on GitHub, go into app, then src, then main, then
  assets. Click "Add file", then "Upload files", drop in the new
  carpe_diem.html (same name), and commit. GitHub builds again by
  itself. You can also start a build by hand: Actions tab, "Build
  Carpe Diem APK", "Run workflow".


IF THE BUILD FAILS
------------------

A red cross that appears within a few seconds, with no steps listed:
  GitHub refused to start the build. The project has not even been
  looked at. This is about the GitHub account, not about Carpe Diem.
  Open the failed run. GitHub shows the reason in a notice near the
  top. The usual reasons are:
    - the account has used its free build minutes for this month
      (private repositories get a monthly allowance), or
    - a spending limit or a payment problem in the account's billing
      settings (github.com/settings/billing).
  Fix what the notice names, then open the run and click
  "Re-run all jobs". You may want to check GitHub's current billing
  rules on its own help pages, as they change from time to time.

A red cross after a few minutes, with steps listed:
  Open the run and click the step with the red cross. The step
  "If the build failed, show why" prints the last 60 lines of the build
  log. Those lines name the cause.

"No settings.gradle found":
  The project files were not uploaded, or only some were. Upload the
  CONTENTS of the "Fire tablet project" folder again.


WHAT IS IN THE PROJECT
----------------------

  app/src/main/assets/carpe_diem.html   The whole app. One file.
  app/src/main/java/.../MainActivity.java  The window it runs in.
  app/carpe-diem.keystore                The permanent signing key.
                                         Keep it. Do not replace it.
  app/build.gradle                       The build settings.
  .github/workflows/build-apk.yml        The recipe GitHub follows.
  GITHUB_WORKFLOW_paste_this.txt         The same recipe, as a visible
                                         file you can copy from.

About the signing key: it proves that each new APK comes from the same
place as the last one, which is what lets an update keep your work.
Its password is written in app/build.gradle. That is acceptable for an
app you install on your own tablet. If you ever publish Carpe Diem in a
store, make a new private key for that and keep it secret.
